Who this is for, up front.

𝗜𝗳 𝘆𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗱𝗮𝘁𝗮 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗰𝗲𝘀𝘀𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗮𝗹𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗱𝘆 𝗹𝗶𝘃𝗲 𝗼𝗻 𝗔𝗪𝗦, 𝗚𝗼𝗼𝗴𝗹𝗲 𝗖𝗹𝗼𝘂𝗱 𝗼𝗿 𝗔𝘇𝘂𝗿𝗲, 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝘀 𝗶𝘀 𝗳𝗼𝗿 𝘆𝗼𝘂.

If you want everything on your own servers with nothing leaving the building, 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗸𝗲𝘆 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗵𝗮𝘀𝗻'𝘁 𝗰𝗵𝗮𝗻𝗴𝗲𝗱. Your agent's records can now stay with you, but every model call still goes out to Anthropic.

Think of it like moving a consultant into your office. Your desk, your files, your notes. But for every decision, they still phone their expert. 𝘕𝘰𝘸 𝘺𝘰𝘶 𝘫𝘶𝘴𝘵 𝘨𝘦𝘵 𝘵𝘰 𝘤𝘩𝘰𝘰𝘴𝘦 𝘸𝘩𝘪𝘤𝘩 𝘱𝘩𝘰𝘯𝘦 𝘭𝘪𝘯𝘦.

𝗛𝗼𝘄 𝗶𝘁 𝗺𝗼𝘃𝗲𝗱, 𝗶𝗻 𝟯 𝗽𝗵𝗮𝘀𝗲𝘀:

• 𝗔𝗽𝗿𝗶𝗹, 𝗠𝗮𝗻𝗮𝗴𝗲𝗱 𝗔𝗴𝗲𝗻𝘁𝘀: Anthropic runs everything. Your documents are uploaded to Anthropic's servers, where the agent opens them and runs its commands. Each time it reads a document, the contents go to the model, which decides the next step, and Anthropic keeps a full record of everything the agent read and did. Quick to start, but your security team has to trust Anthropic with your documents, the work done on them and the record of it all.
𝘛𝘩𝘦 𝘤𝘰𝘯𝘴𝘶𝘭𝘵𝘢𝘯𝘵 𝘵𝘢𝘬𝘦𝘴 𝘤𝘰𝘱𝘪𝘦𝘴 𝘰𝘧 𝘺𝘰𝘶𝘳 𝘧𝘪𝘭𝘦𝘴 𝘣𝘢𝘤𝘬 𝘵𝘰 𝘵𝘩𝘦𝘪𝘳 𝘰𝘸𝘯 𝘰𝘧𝘧𝘪𝘤𝘦, 𝘢𝘯𝘥 𝘵𝘩𝘦𝘪𝘳 𝘧𝘪𝘳𝘮 𝘬𝘦𝘦𝘱𝘴 𝘵𝘩𝘦 𝘯𝘰𝘵𝘦𝘴.

• 𝗠𝗮𝘆, 𝘀𝗲𝗹𝗳-𝗵𝗼𝘀𝘁𝗲𝗱 𝘀𝗮𝗻𝗱𝗯𝗼𝘅𝗲𝘀: the tools move to infrastructure you control, your cloud account or your own servers. The agent opens your documents and runs commands there, next to your data. But every time it reads a document, the contents are sent back to Anthropic, which decides the next step and keeps a full record of everything the agent read and did. So your documents are opened on your side, but what's in them still ends up stored by Anthropic.
𝘛𝘩𝘦 𝘤𝘰𝘯𝘴𝘶𝘭𝘵𝘢𝘯𝘵 𝘴𝘪𝘵𝘴 𝘢𝘵 𝘺𝘰𝘶𝘳 𝘥𝘦𝘴𝘬, 𝘣𝘶𝘵 𝘳𝘦𝘢𝘥𝘴 𝘦𝘢𝘤𝘩 𝘱𝘢𝘨𝘦 𝘢𝘭𝘰𝘶𝘥 𝘵𝘰 𝘵𝘩𝘦𝘪𝘳 𝘧𝘪𝘳𝘮, 𝘸𝘩𝘪𝘤𝘩 𝘬𝘦𝘦𝘱𝘴 𝘵𝘩𝘦 𝘯𝘰𝘵𝘦𝘴.

• 𝗡𝗼𝘄, 𝘀𝗲𝗹𝗳-𝗵𝗼𝘀𝘁𝗲𝗱 𝗠𝗮𝗻𝗮𝗴𝗲𝗱 𝗔𝗴𝗲𝗻𝘁𝘀: everything runs on infrastructure you control, your cloud account or your own data centre. The agent opens your documents there, runs each step there, and keeps the full record of what it read and did in your own database. One thing still leaves: each time the agent needs to think, what it has read so far is sent to the model. You choose where that goes: Anthropic, or Claude through Bedrock, Google Cloud or Azure. On Bedrock, AWS says model providers can't see prompts or completions, so Anthropic doesn't see your documents. On Azure, that holds only for the Azure-hosted Claude deployment.

𝗪𝗵𝘆 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝘀 𝗼𝗿𝗱𝗲𝗿: the way I read it, each step answered the next objection from security teams. First "run it next to our data", then "keep our records with us", then "don't make us vet one more company".

𝗪𝗵𝗼 𝗴𝗮𝗶𝗻𝘀 𝗺𝗼𝘀𝘁: companies that already trust one cloud. A bank with its data in S3 can keep the agent, its records and its model calls inside AWS. No new vendor to approve. Retention follows that provider, so read the terms for Claude there.
