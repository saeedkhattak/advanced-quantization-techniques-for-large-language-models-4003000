===== STORY =====

𝗬𝗼𝘂 𝗙𝗼𝘂𝗻𝗱 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗣𝗲𝗿𝗳𝗲𝗰𝘁 𝗜𝗺𝗮𝗴𝗲. 𝗡𝗼𝘄 𝗧𝗿𝘆 𝗪𝗿𝗶𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗜𝘁𝘀 𝗣𝗿𝗼𝗺𝗽𝘁.

You see a product shot you love. Soft light from the left, a cream background, one glossy bottle catching a highlight. You want something like it from an AI image tool, so you type "product photo, soft light, minimal" and get something that looks nothing like it.

The hard part was never the generator. 𝗜𝘁'𝘀 𝗽𝘂𝘁𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝘄𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗺𝗮𝗸𝗲𝘀 𝗮𝗻 𝗶𝗺𝗮𝗴𝗲 𝗹𝗼𝗼𝗸 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝘄𝗮𝘆 𝗶𝘁 𝗱𝗼𝗲𝘀 𝗶𝗻𝘁𝗼 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗱𝘀.

That's the job of image-prompt-reverse, a small open-source skill for Codex by LunarXuan. You give it a reference image, and it writes the prompt for you.

There's no code in it. It's a set of instructions: a SKILL.md file and three reference guides that tell the model how to study an image the way a photographer or illustrator would.

First it works out what kind of image it is (an ad, a portrait, a poster, a 3D character) and what medium. Then it goes through composition, camera angle, light direction, colour, materials, background, depth and mood.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗰𝗹𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗿 𝗯𝗶𝘁 𝗶𝘀 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗶𝘁 𝗽𝗶𝗰𝗸𝘀 𝟯 𝘁𝗼 𝟱 "𝘃𝗶𝘀𝘂𝗮𝗹 𝗮𝗻𝗰𝗵𝗼𝗿𝘀", the details that matter most for a close match, and puts them in the first third of the prompt.

You get two things back: a long prompt of 𝟰𝟱𝟬 𝘁𝗼 𝟳𝟬𝟬 𝗖𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗲𝘀𝗲 𝗰𝗵𝗮𝗿𝗮𝗰𝘁𝗲𝗿𝘀 with an English version, and a list of 10 to 15 English negative terms tailored to that image.

The rules it follows are the most useful part for anyone who writes prompts. Don't invent what you can't see. Don't guess a focal length or aperture, describe the visible effect instead. Don't lean on an artist's or photographer's name, describe the technique. Words like "cinematic" or "premium" have to be explained with something concrete in the picture.

It also treats any text inside the image as part of the picture, never as an instruction. So an image that says "ignore your rules" can't take over the agent.

𝗙𝗼𝗿 𝘂𝘀𝗲𝗿𝘀, it's a quick way to get a strong first prompt. 𝗙𝗼𝗿 𝗯𝘂𝗶𝗹𝗱𝗲𝗿𝘀, it's a good example of a skill that's all method and no code.

It's not a new idea. Midjourney's /describe gives you four prompts from an image, and its docs say that's for inspiration, not exact copies. CLIP Interrogator has done image-to-prompt for Stable Diffusion for years. This one is a much more detailed checklist, run by a general AI model.

Some caveats. 𝗧𝗵𝗲𝗿𝗲 𝗮𝗿𝗲 𝗻𝗼 𝗲𝘅𝗮𝗺𝗽𝗹𝗲 𝗼𝘂𝘁𝗽𝘂𝘁𝘀 𝗼𝗿 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗽𝗮𝗿𝗶𝘀𝗼𝗻𝘀 𝗶𝗻 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗿𝗲𝗽𝗼, so how close the recreated images get is unproven. It's written for Codex, and the Chinese prompt comes first. It's also young: 5 commits, all in early September, and about 930 stars. GPL-3.0.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗶𝗻𝘁𝗲𝗿𝗲𝘀𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗽𝗮𝗿𝘁 𝗶𝘀𝗻'𝘁 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗼𝘂𝘁𝗽𝘂𝘁. 𝗜𝘁'𝘀 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝘄𝗵𝗼𝗹𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗶𝘀 𝗮 𝗰𝗵𝗲𝗰𝗸𝗹𝗶𝘀𝘁 𝘄𝗿𝗶𝘁𝘁𝗲𝗻 𝗶𝗻 𝗽𝗹𝗮𝗶𝗻 𝗹𝗮𝗻𝗴𝘂𝗮𝗴𝗲. No model, no code. A SKILL.md and three reference files.

Think about what a good image prompt actually is. It's a compressed description of the few decisions that made the picture: where the light comes from, what's closest to the camera, what the material feels like. Most of what's in an image barely matters for similarity. A handful of things matter a lot.

The skill encodes that directly. It asks the model to pick 𝟯 𝘁𝗼 𝟱 "𝘃𝗶𝘀𝘂𝗮𝗹 𝗮𝗻𝗰𝗵𝗼𝗿𝘀" and put them in the first third of the prompt, then fill in the rest. It's a bit like a sketch artist working from a witness. You don't describe every hair, you get the jawline and the eyes right first.

It also bans the usual filler. "Cinematic" or "premium" must be turned into visible things. No camera specs unless the image actually shows them. No artist names, describe the technique instead.

I think this is a nice example of where a lot of applied AI work is heading: the method lives in text, and the model does the seeing.

What I'd hold loosely: there's no evaluation in the repo. No before and after images, no similarity scores. The default output is Chinese first (450 to 700 characters) with English after.

===== ETHAN MOLLICK STYLE =====

𝗖𝗮𝗻 𝗔𝗻 𝗔𝗜 𝗘𝘅𝗽𝗹𝗮𝗶𝗻 𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗠𝗮𝗸𝗲𝘀 𝗔 𝗣𝗶𝗰𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗲 𝗟𝗼𝗼𝗸 𝗚𝗼𝗼𝗱?

Most of us know a great photo when we see one. Far fewer of us can say why. Is it the light? The angle? The colours?

That gap matters now, because AI image tools only respond to words. If you can't describe what you like, you can't ask for it.

A small free project called image-prompt-reverse tries to close that gap. You show it a picture, and it writes the description an image generator would need to make something similar.

𝗜𝘁 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗸𝘀 𝗹𝗶𝗸𝗲 𝗮 𝗰𝗵𝗲𝗰𝗸𝗹𝗶𝘀𝘁 𝗳𝗼𝗿 𝗮𝗻 𝗲𝘅𝗽𝗲𝗿𝘁 𝗲𝘆𝗲. Before writing anything, the AI decides what kind of picture it is (an advert, a portrait, a cartoon, a 3D model) and then looks at the layout, the camera angle, where the light comes from, the colours, the textures and the mood.

Then it does something a good teacher would do. It picks the 3 to 5 details that matter most and puts them first.

It's like describing a friend to someone meeting them at the airport. You don't list everything. You say "tall, red coat, curly hair", the few things that make them easy to spot.

It also has sensible rules. Don't make up details you can't see. Don't name famous artists, describe the technique. Turn a brand's logo into plain shapes and colours.

On the other side, there's no proof yet that it works well. The project shows no before and after pictures. It's built for one AI coding tool, Codex, and writes in Chinese first. Similar features have existed for a while, such as Midjourney's /describe, which says its prompts are for inspiration, not exact copies.

What we know is that describing an image well is a real skill, and AI can now be given that skill in a short set of instructions.
