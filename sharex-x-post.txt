===== 1. STORY =====

𝗔 𝟭𝟵-𝗬𝗲𝗮𝗿-𝗢𝗹𝗱 𝗦𝗰𝗿𝗲𝗲𝗻𝘀𝗵𝗼𝘁 𝗧𝗼𝗼𝗹 𝗝𝘂𝘀𝘁 𝗥𝗲𝘄𝗿𝗼𝘁𝗲 𝗜𝘁𝘀 𝗪𝗵𝗼𝗹𝗲 𝗨𝗜

You take a screenshot. Then you open another app to draw an arrow on it, another to blur the email address, and a browser tab to upload it and copy the link.

𝗦𝗵𝗮𝗿𝗲𝗫 𝗱𝗼𝗲𝘀 𝗮𝗹𝗹 𝗼𝗳 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗳𝗿𝗼𝗺 𝗼𝗻𝗲 𝗵𝗼𝘁𝗸𝗲𝘆, and it has been doing it on Windows for 19 years.

It's free and open source (GPL-3.0). The README says no ads and no account needed.

𝗛𝗼𝘄 𝗶𝘁 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗸𝘀

You pick a capture type: region, window, full screen, scrolling page, video or GIF.

Then you tell it what happens next. Open the editor, save the file, upload it, shorten the link, copy the link to the clipboard. ShareX calls these 𝘢𝘧𝘵𝘦𝘳-𝘤𝘢𝘱𝘵𝘶𝘳𝘦 𝘵𝘢𝘴𝘬𝘴, and you can chain them.

===== 2. KARPATHY STYLE =====

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗦𝗰𝗿𝗲𝗲𝗻𝘀𝗵𝗼𝘁 𝗜𝘀 𝗡𝗼𝘁 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗣𝗿𝗼𝗱𝘂𝗰𝘁. 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗣𝗶𝗽𝗲𝗹𝗶𝗻𝗲 𝗜𝘀.

Been reading the ShareX changelog. It's a 19-year-old open source screenshot tool for Windows, about 41k stars on GitHub, GPL-3.0.

Most people file it under "better Snipping Tool". I think that undersells it.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗶𝗻𝘁𝗲𝗿𝗲𝘀𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗽𝗮𝗿𝘁 𝗶𝘀 𝘄𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗵𝗮𝗽𝗽𝗲𝗻𝘀 𝗮𝗳𝘁𝗲𝗿 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗰𝗮𝗽𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗲. You define a chain: capture region, annotate, save, upload to your own bucket, shorten the URL, copy the link. One hotkey runs the lot.

It's a bit like a Unix pipe for pixels. The capture is just stdin.

That design explains why the tool list keeps growing. OCR, QR scanning, a hash checker, a video trimmer, a remote storage browser. The README lists 𝟯𝟳 𝗼𝗳 𝘁𝗵𝗲𝗺. Each one is another stage you can put in the pipe.

The October release (22.0.0) is the part I find brave. They rewrote 𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗿𝘆 𝗽𝗮𝗿𝘁 𝗼𝗳 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗨𝗜 𝗳𝗿𝗼𝗺 𝘀𝗰𝗿𝗮𝘁𝗰𝗵 and said plainly they expect it to be "𝘴𝘪𝘨𝘯𝘪𝘧𝘪𝘤𝘢𝘯𝘵𝘭𝘺 𝘭𝘦𝘴𝘴 𝘴𝘵𝘢𝘣𝘭𝘦 𝘵𝘩𝘢𝘯 𝘰𝘶𝘳 𝘶𝘴𝘶𝘢𝘭 𝘳𝘦𝘭𝘦𝘢𝘴𝘦𝘴". Then they shipped four more versions in under three weeks.

A few details I liked:

• 𝗥𝗲𝗰𝗼𝗿𝗱𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝘄𝗶𝘁𝗵𝗼𝘂𝘁 𝗙𝗙𝗺𝗽𝗲𝗴: native capture with system audio, mic and camera overlay, FPS limit raised to 𝟭𝟮𝟬
• 𝗦𝗶𝗴𝗻𝗲𝗱 𝗯𝘂𝗶𝗹𝗱𝘀: installer and assemblies are now code-signed
• 𝗔 𝘄𝗮𝘆 𝗯𝗮𝗰𝗸: 22.4.0 added a "Compact mode" preset and a Ruler that works like v21

That last one matters imo. A rewrite of a 19-year-old tool breaks a lot of muscle memory, and they're adding the old behaviour back as options instead of arguing with users.

Limits: 𝗪𝗶𝗻𝗱𝗼𝘄𝘀 𝗼𝗻𝗹𝘆, and v22 is very new. If your workflow depends on it, I'd keep the previous build around for a few weeks.

===== 4. SWYX STYLE =====

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗕𝗼𝗿𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗦𝗰𝗿𝗲𝗲𝗻𝘀𝗵𝗼𝘁 𝗧𝗼𝗼𝗹 𝗧𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗜𝘀 𝗦𝘁𝗶𝗹𝗹 𝗛𝗲𝗿𝗲 𝗔𝗳𝘁𝗲𝗿 𝟭𝟵 𝗬𝗲𝗮𝗿𝘀

There's a category I'd call capture-to-link: press a key, get a URL you can paste. It looks trivial and people keep building it.

Roughly how the options sit today:

• 𝗦𝗻𝗶𝗽𝗽𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗧𝗼𝗼𝗹: ships with Windows, fine for a quick grab
• 𝗚𝗿𝗲𝗲𝗻𝘀𝗵𝗼𝘁: open source, usually described as the simpler choice
• 𝗙𝗹𝗮𝗺𝗲𝘀𝗵𝗼𝘁: open source and cross-platform, the usual Linux answer
• 𝗦𝗵𝗮𝗿𝗲𝗫: open source, Windows only, the power-user end

ShareX is the one with staying power. 𝟭𝟵 𝘆𝗲𝗮𝗿𝘀 𝗼𝗹𝗱, 𝗮𝗯𝗼𝘂𝘁 𝟰𝟭𝗸 𝗚𝗶𝘁𝗛𝘂𝗯 𝘀𝘁𝗮𝗿𝘀, 𝗚𝗣𝗟-𝟯.𝟬, no ads and no account according to its README.

It doesn't win on the capture. It wins on the workflow layer on top: after-capture tasks, after-upload tasks, custom uploaders, hotkeys per workflow. Capture, annotate, upload to your own bucket, shorten, copy. One key.

And in October it did the thing old projects almost never do. Version 22.0.0 𝗿𝗲𝘄𝗿𝗼𝘁𝗲 𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗿𝘆 𝗽𝗮𝗿𝘁 𝗼𝗳 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗨𝗜, added screen recording that doesn't rely on FFmpeg (system audio, mic, camera overlay, up to 120 FPS), moved to .NET 10 and started code-signing its builds.

Then 22.1 through 22.4 landed in under three weeks: a layer-based photo editor, WebP, HDR tone mapping, and a compact mode for people who liked v21.

The risk is real. The team said it expects v22 to be "𝘴𝘪𝘨𝘯𝘪𝘧𝘪𝘤𝘢𝘯𝘵𝘭𝘺 𝘭𝘦𝘴𝘴 𝘴𝘵𝘢𝘣𝘭𝘦 𝘵𝘩𝘢𝘯 𝘰𝘶𝘳 𝘶𝘴𝘶𝘢𝘭 𝘳𝘦𝘭𝘦𝘢𝘴𝘦𝘴", and it's still 𝗪𝗶𝗻𝗱𝗼𝘄𝘀 𝗼𝗻𝗹𝘆.

My read: the durable desktop tools are the ones that turn a single action into a programmable pipeline. Screenshots were just the entry point.

===== 6. ETHAN MOLLICK STYLE =====

𝗪𝗵𝘆 𝗗𝗼𝗲𝘀 𝗔 𝗙𝗿𝗲𝗲 𝗦𝗰𝗿𝗲𝗲𝗻𝘀𝗵𝗼𝘁 𝗔𝗽𝗽 𝗛𝗮𝘃𝗲 𝟰𝟭,𝟬𝟬𝟬 𝗦𝘁𝗮𝗿𝘀?

Most of us take a screenshot the same way we did ten years ago. Press a key, paste it somewhere, then realise we forgot to hide something private.

ShareX is a free, open source Windows app that treats this as a small assembly line. You press one key. It captures the area, lets you draw on it, blurs what you choose, uploads it and puts the link in your clipboard.

𝗜𝘁 𝗵𝗮𝘀 𝗯𝗲𝗲𝗻 𝗶𝗻 𝗱𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗹𝗼𝗽𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁 𝗳𝗼𝗿 𝟭𝟵 𝘆𝗲𝗮𝗿𝘀, and it has about 41,000 stars on GitHub (a rough sign of how many developers have bookmarked it).

This autumn the team did something unusual for a project that old. They rewrote the whole interface. Their own words: "𝘵𝘩𝘦 𝘣𝘪𝘨𝘨𝘦𝘴𝘵 𝘶𝘱𝘥𝘢𝘵𝘦 𝘸𝘦'𝘷𝘦 𝘦𝘷𝘦𝘳 𝘳𝘦𝘭𝘦𝘢𝘴𝘦𝘥."

What's new, in plain terms:

• 𝗩𝗶𝗱𝗲𝗼: it can now record your screen with sound from the computer and your microphone, and show your webcam in the corner
• 𝗘𝗱𝗶𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴: there's a photo editor with layers, like a small version of the tools designers use
• 𝗘𝘅𝘁𝗿𝗮𝘀: 37 small utilities are listed, such as pulling text out of an image or reading a QR code

There are two sides to this. A big rewrite brings a modern design. It also breaks habits, and the team said openly that this version would be "𝘴𝘪𝘨𝘯𝘪𝘧𝘪𝘤𝘢𝘯𝘵𝘭𝘺 𝘭𝘦𝘴𝘴 𝘴𝘵𝘢𝘣𝘭𝘦" than usual. They have shipped four updates since, and one brings back the old spacing for people who preferred it.

What we actually know: it's free, has no ads, needs no account, and 𝗼𝗻𝗹𝘆 𝗿𝘂𝗻𝘀 𝗼𝗻 𝗪𝗶𝗻𝗱𝗼𝘄𝘀. What we don't have is an outside measurement of how fast or light it is.
