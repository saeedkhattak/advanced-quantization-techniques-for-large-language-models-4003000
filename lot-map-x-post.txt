𝗔 𝗟𝗶𝘃𝗲 𝗠𝗮𝗽 𝗼𝗳 𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗖𝗹𝗮𝘂𝗱𝗲 𝗖𝗼𝗱𝗲 𝗜𝘀 𝗗𝗼𝗶𝗻𝗴

You give Claude Code a task and the terminal fills with lines. Read, Grep, Bash, Edit, a subagent, another Bash. By the time you've scrolled back, it's done ten more things.

It's a small mod that opens 𝗮 𝗹𝗶𝘃𝗲 𝗺𝗮𝗽 𝗶𝗻 𝗮 𝗽𝗮𝗻𝗲 𝗯𝗲𝘀𝗶𝗱𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗰𝗵𝗮𝘁. In the middle is the agent. Around it are stations: You, Model, Memory, Files, Shell, Network, Browser, Agents, Skills and Hooks.

• 𝗥𝗲𝗮𝗱𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗼𝗿 𝗲𝗱𝗶𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗰𝗼𝗱𝗲 lights up Files

• 𝗦𝘂𝗯𝗮𝗴𝗲𝗻𝘁𝘀 show up as small dots circling the Agents station

A simple way to get value from both:

1. Keep lot-map open while the agent works, and watch for red pulses and the context ring.
2. When something looks off, note the time and the station.
3. Open the full session log and read the calls around that moment.
4. Write down what went wrong, and turn it into a test case for next time.

===== 6. ETHAN MOLLICK STYLE =====

𝗖𝗮𝗻 𝗬𝗼𝘂 𝗦𝗲𝗲 𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗬𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗔𝗜 𝗜𝘀 𝗗𝗼𝗶𝗻𝗴?

AI agents now do real work on our computers. They read files, run commands and visit websites, often faster than anyone can follow.

Most of the time we only see a stream of technical text. That's a bit like supervising someone by reading their receipts after they've gone shopping.

A new free add-on for Claude Code called 𝗹𝗼𝘁-𝗺𝗮𝗽 tries something different. It draws 𝗮 𝗹𝗶𝘃𝗲 𝗺𝗮𝗽 beside the chat, a bit like a subway map. The AI sits in the middle, and stations around it stand for things like files, the internet, memory and helper agents. Each action sends a pulse to the right station, and mistakes flash red.

It even shows a gauge for how full the AI's working memory is getting.

There's a reasonable case for this. People trust what they can see, and a picture is easier to follow than a scrolling log.
